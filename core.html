Core Security Principles

Quick refresher

Start with what must be protected and what failure would cost. Confidentiality limits disclosure, integrity preserves correctness, and availability keeps a service usable. Least privilege limits what a principal can do. Defense in depth combines controls whose failures are as independent as possible.

Worked example

For payroll, encryption protects stored data, scoped authorization limits who can read records, and tested backups protect availability. An audit trail helps investigate changes after the fact.

Interview practice

How would you prioritize controls for a payroll service?

Identify the sensitive data and critical workflows, then estimate the likelihood and business impact of failures. Choose preventive and detective controls, assign an owner, and verify each control with evidence.
